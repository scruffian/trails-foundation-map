# Naming and language

## The organisation name

**Working name: Dig Trails**

The name combines two things:

- **Dig** — the authentic insider term for the act of building and maintaining trails. "Going for a dig" is part of the culture. It's active, physical, and rooted in the DIY ethic.
- **Trails** — the insider term for the discipline itself. Within BMX and serious MTB dirt jumping circles, the sport is called trails, not dirt jumping. Using this signals the organisation is of the community, not external to it.

The name has not yet been:

- Checked against domain availability (digtrails.org.uk, digtrails.co.uk)
- Checked against Charity Commission register
- Checked against Companies House
- Tested with the community

These checks should happen before the name is committed to publicly.

## Considered alternatives

- **Dig Trust / Dig Foundation** — uses charitable terminology more explicitly. Foundation might be a better fit because it describes the grant-distributing function of the organisation.
- **Dig Collective** — emphasises the community of people rather than the places. Closer to the recognition-of-people aspect of the mission.
- **Trails Trust / Trails Foundation** — more outsider-legible but less culturally authentic.

A possible compromise: **Dig Trails** as the public brand and website name, **Dig Trails Foundation** as the legal charity name.

## Language conventions

### Always use

- **Trails** — for the discipline (e.g. "a trails spot", "trails community")
- **Dig / digging** — for the act of building and maintaining
- **Riders** — for the people who use the spots
- **Spot** — for a single location
- **Line** — for a single sequence of jumps within a spot

### Use carefully

- **Dirt jumping** — acceptable as an explanatory term when communicating with outsiders (funders, councils, journalists). Should not be used in community-facing content.
- **Facility** — fine in formal / institutional contexts. Avoid in rider-facing copy where "spot" is more natural.
- **Mountain biking** — context matters. Trails sits within MTB but isn't synonymous with it.

### Avoid

- **Extreme sport** — riders find this dated and embarrassing.
- **BMX freestyle** — only use when specifically referring to that discipline. Trails is shared between MTB and BMX but has its own identity.
- **Track** — implies a fixed loop. Trails are usually lines.

## Tone

- Direct, not performative
- Confident, not corporate
- Respectful of community knowledge
- Not condescending to outsiders, but doesn't pander to them either

## Visual / brand

To be decided. Working principle: not generic, not corporate, not over-stylised "extreme sport". Should feel like the community made it.
